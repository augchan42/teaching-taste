Thesis

What Is Taste?

Taste is the essence of what you like, but hard to explain in words. A person's taste is usually best defined through prolonged observation.

How do you teach something you can only recognize — never fully articulate — to a machine?

The Iterative Loop

Human Judges Output
"Like this, not that"
→
AI Extracts & Encodes Rules
Figures out why, updates the skill

Re-run. Judge again. Repeat.

You can't reason about the model. You can only judge its output.

Round 1: Style as Suffix

"…overflowing well, water branching… Ink wash with soft watercolor tints."

The model treated "ink wash" as a filter, not a language.

Round 2: Try a Different Style?

"…overflowing well… Wabi-sabi aesthetic, muted tones."

It's a photograph. The style suffix changed the medium.

Core Discovery

Style Is Not a Suffix

Write it like the caption under the painting — not a scene plus a style suffix.

# Bad — style as suffix "A tree on a hill. Chinese ink painting style." # Good — painterly language embedded in the scene "A gnarled pine leans from a cliff face like a brushstroke frozen mid-sweep, roots gripping stone above a gorge filled with morning mist. Chinese ink painting."

Same verse. First time it worked.

What the AI Analyzed

The human said “this works.” The AI figured out why. Four of the seven rules came out of that analysis.

Painterly words produce paintings RULE 7

Language register controls the medium. Photographic words produce photographs.

Force depth RULE 2

Foreground/midground/background forces the model to compose, not just fill.

Warm accent RULE 4

A single point of color in monochrome gives the eye a destination.

Frame the scene RULE 1

Gates, tree canopies, gorge walls — never a subject floating in open space.

Rules 1, 2, 4 and 7 — visible in one image

What the Human Kept

The AI distilled these from human preferences:

7 Composition Rules

  1. 1. Frame the scene
  2. 2. Force depth
  3. 3. Demand contrast
  4. 4. Warm accent
  5. 5. Favor diagonals
  6. 6. Discoverable detail
  7. 7. Painterly language

# Style table fallback for abstract verses: If abstract, choose by tone: ominous → atmospheric-night auspicious → ink-landscape / figures-in-mist mixed → bold-action # All 7 composition rules still apply.

Agent output

Tone: serene, auspicious → ink-landscape
Frame: valley at sunset, stone steps through a temple gate
Depth: worn steps (fg) → wanderer on path (mid) → hazy peaks (bg)
Accent: golden-hour light on the gate

The rules require concrete visual elements even when the verse supplies none.

Takeaways

Style is not a suffix

Language register controls the medium. Painterly words produce paintings; appending "ink painting style" produces a filter.

Taste is iterative, not declarative

You can't define good output upfront. It emerges through cycles of human judgment and AI analysis.

Humans judge; the AI articulates

You say "this one." The AI figures out why it works and writes the rule down — where you can check it.

Encode the preference, not the person.
